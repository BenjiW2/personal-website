Why use baited remote underwater video?

Baited Remote Underwater Videos (BRUVs) are widely used to study the relative abundance, behaviour and biodiversity of marine fauna (Grimmel et al., 2020; Kiggins et al., 2020; Langlois et al., 2020). They are a non-invasive, low-cost method that lets us study fish assemblages in different habitats (French et al., 2021; Gilby et al., 2016; Jones et al., 2019; Kiggins et al., 2020).

Willis et al. (2000) showed that BRUVs can be even more effective than underwater visual census for some species, because they remove the human disturbance that makes diver-shy species avoid divers. The bait plume also attracts fish from a wider area than a visual survey transect can cover (Willis et al., 2000).

BRUVs do have limitations, such as a bias towards attracting predatory and scavenging fishes. However, Harvey et al. (2007) showed that this does not stop herbivorous fishes from visiting, and that baited videos are overall more effective than unbaited videos (RUVs).

What we're doing today

In this workshop we will analyse BRUV videos that we collected in different habitats of Quandamooka. You will watch BRUV videos, identify fish species, and count how many of each species you see. We will record MaxN: the maximum number of individuals of a species visible in a single frame, rather than tallying every sighting. Everyone's counts are collated as you go, and by the end of the session we will combine our observations to compare assemblages between habitats.

Your task
  1. ID guide: get to know the species you might see. Filter by habitat or search by name.
  2. Enter data: type your name or group, then choose the habitat, station and video. It plays in the app, or open it in a new tab.
  3. For each species, find the moment when the most individuals are visible at once and record that number (MaxN). Leave it at 0 if you don't see it.
  4. Submit, then pick your next video.
  5. Graphs: compare everyone's results between habitats, sites or any other category.
MaxN tip: pause the video when a school is biggest and count in that frame. Never add counts from different moments together, because the same fish may swim in and out of view many times.
